## 2. Exact wording (verbatim; identical in v1, v2, v3 and the Aug-2026 draft; section "1. Problems of 2024"; attribution rendered "[Wor]" in arXiv HTML, "[Dale R. Worley]" in the PDFs)

Problem 2: "Consider the tree SSYT of shifted standard Young tableaux, with the tree structure generated by containment. Computation suggests that the only automorphism of SSYT (as an unlabeled tree) is the identity. Computation further suggests that each node of SSYT is uniquely determined by the census (cardinalities) of the ranks of the subtree rooted at the node."

Problem 3: "Consider the tree SYT of (unshifted) standard Young tableaux, with the tree structure generated by containment. For any node with a symmetric shape, there is an automorphism of SYT (as an unlabeled tree) that transposes all nodes in the subtree rooted at the node and leaves all other nodes fixed. Computation suggests that all automorphisms of SYT are compositions of these transpose automorphisms (with the identity being the composition of zero transpose automorphisms). Computation further suggests that each node of SYT is determined up to transpose by the census (cardinalities) of the ranks of the subtree rooted at the node."

Definition of the partial-transpose automorphism: one automorphism per NODE whose shape is symmetric (self-conjugate); it acts on the WHOLE subtree rooted at that node ("transposes all nodes in the subtree rooted at the node") and fixes every node outside it. It is NOT defined per mirror pair of children. All four reports agree on this reading.

Notes on the text itself:
- "SSYT" here means shifted standard (not semistandard) tableaux.
- The notebook never says what "transposes" means for a descendant beyond that sentence, gives no definition of "census" beyond "(cardinalities) of the ranks of the subtree rooted at the node", no figure, no proof, and no computational range.
- Interpretation (Report 1's, NOT in the notebook): the literal map U -> U^t cannot be meant, since a tableau with >= 2 entries is never its own transpose and the root node itself would move; the only consistent reading is: keep the symmetric-shape tableau T fixed and transpose the skew part U / sh(T) of every descendant U. Also, since infinitely many pairwise non-nested symmetric-shape nodes can be flipped simultaneously, "compositions" presumably must allow infinite locally-finite products (cf. the YF record's caution above).

Related problems in the same notebook (verbatim, arXiv v3): "8. [Wor] Consider the tree k-SYT of standard Young tableaux with at most k rows, with the tree structure generated by containment. What are its automorphisms, when it is considered an unlabeled tree?" and "9. [Wor] Consider the tree SYFT of standard 'Young–Fibonacci tableaux', with the tree structure generated by containment, or equivalently, the tree of upward paths in the Young–Fibonacci lattice. What are the automorphisms of this tree, when it is considered an unlabeled tree?" In the Aug-2026 draft these carry the solution notes: Problem 8 "[OpCod2026a] via [JonLuca DeCaro] proves that the only automorphism is the identity." ## 3. References for the census generating-function identity (verified by Report 4 only)

The census of the subtree below a node of shape lambda (|lambda| = k) is N(n; lambda) = sum_{mu |- n} f^{mu/lambda} (depends only on lambda; noted by all four reports). The identity sum_n (u^n/n!) N(n+k; lambda) = e^{u + u^2/2} sum_j (u^j/j!) d_j(lambda), with d_j(lambda) = sum_{nu |- k-j} f^{lambda/nu}, is published:

- R. P. Stanley, "On the enumeration of skew Young tableaux", Adv. in Appl. Math. 30 (2003) 283–294, arXiv:math/0106115. Theorem 2.2, eqs. (9) and (10); eq. (10) is exactly the identity (verified from pymupdf text). Proof: exponential specialization of the skew Littlewood identity, which Stanley cites as "[6, Exam. I.5.27(a), p. 93][12, Exer. 7.27(e)]" with bijective proof "Sagan and Stanley [11, Cor. 6.4]", crediting "Lascoux, Macdonald, Towber, Stanley, Zelevinsky, and perhaps others". Stanley also notes N(n; alpha) = N(n; alpha') — consistent with Worley's "up to transpose".
- I. G. Macdonald, Symmetric Functions and Hall Polynomials, 2nd ed. (1995), Ch. I, Sec. 5, Example 27(a), formula (3), p. 93: sum_rho s_{rho/lambda} = prod_i (1-x_i)^{-1} prod_{i<j} (1-x_i x_j)^{-1} sum_tau s_{lambda/tau}. Verified from a scanned copy (OCR garbles indices) and independently confirmed by Stanley 2003's citation. Example 12 (p. 80) is the exponential specialization giving exp(t + t^2/2) (involutions). First-edition (1979) numbering NOT checked.
- B. E. Sagan, R. P. Stanley, "Robinson-Schensted algorithms for skew tableaux", JCTA 55 (1990) 161–193: Cor. 3.5 (coefficient form: sum_{lambda/alpha |- n} f^{lambda/alpha} = sum_k C(n,k) Inv(k) sum_{alpha/mu |- n-k} f^{alpha/mu}), Cor. 3.7 (refinement tracking fixed points / odd columns), Cor. 6.4 (bijective proof of the symmetric-function identity). Read via tesseract OCR of a scan — formulas approximate.
- R. P. Stanley, "Differential posets", J. Amer. Math. Soc. 1 (1988) 919–961: Cor. 2.6(c) e^{Dt} P~ = exp(rt + r t^2/2 + Ut) P~; Prop. 3.1 eqs. (12)–(13); Thm 3.2 eqs. (16)–(17); eq. (21). Also EC1 2nd ed. (2012) Sec. 3.21, Thm 3.21.9 eq. (3.107), Thms 3.21.10–3.21.11. IMPORTANT: the fixed-start-vertex version of the identity is NOT written out explicitly in Stanley 1988 or EC1 (everything there is for x = 0-hat, rank-generating functions, or walks ending at x); it is an immediate corollary of Cor. 2.6(c) by pairing with a fixed x. Stanley 1988 read via OCR.
- Numerical check (Report 4, Python): identity holds for lambda in {(), (1), (2), (1,1), (2,1), (3,1), (2,2), (3,2,1), (4,2,1), (3,3), (5,3,2,1)}, k <= 8. Report 3 independently hand-verified it for mu = (1), (2) via s_mu^perp.
- NOT verified: EC2 Exercise 7.27(e) text (EC2 not opened); Fomin's dual-graded-graph papers not checked for a fixed-vertex statement.

Consequence for the problems: the (infinite) upward census of a node of shape lambda is equivalent to the finite vector (d_0(lambda), ..., d_k(lambda)) of "number of ways to peel j boxes off lambda". So the census half of Problem 3 is equivalent to: the vector (sum_{nu subset lambda, |lambda/nu| = j} f^{lambda/nu})_j determines lambda up to conjugation; Problem 2 asks the shifted analogue outright. No report found any source addressing this injectivity question or any name for the statistic d_j. Sample values (Report 4, Python; I spot-checked d(3,2,1) by hand and it is correct): d(3,2,1) = [1,3,6,10,16,16,16], d(4,2,1) = [1,3,7,14,23,35,35,35]. Totals over lambda |- n (1, 2, 6, 15, 42, 115, 344, 1057, 3483, 11925, 43129) are not in OEIS.
